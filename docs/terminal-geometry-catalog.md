# Terminal geometry catalog from the real-airport corpus

## Reading convention

This is a descriptive visual spec derived from the SVGs in `reference/real-airports/`.
It describes what the chart depicts, not a current gate inventory or an architectural
survey. Counts use **named or visually distinct passenger processors** as terminals.
Attached and detached concourses are described under their processor but are not added to
the terminal count. Where a publisher labels sectors of one continuous building as
terminals, the entry states both the sector count and the physical continuity. A count of
zero means that no passenger-terminal footprint can be identified confidently in the
chart; hangars, military buildings, and generic GA facilities are not reclassified as
terminals.

The categories are primary morphologies, not exclusive types: **linear**, **curvilinear
linear**, **pier/comb**, **satellite/midfield**, **unit terminal**, and **hybrid**. The real
diagrams repeatedly show why a large airport needs more than one category.

The checked-out corpus contains 71 SVGs. Its manifest also names FAOR and FALE, but those
two files are absent and therefore have no entry below.

## FAA — United States

### KATL — Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International

- **Chart count:** 2 passenger processors: the Domestic Terminal and Maynard H. Jackson
  Jr. International Terminal. **Category:** two-ended processor plus parallel midfield
  satellite system; overall a hybrid.
- **Domestic Terminal outline:** a broad, blunt processor at the west end, split into
  north and south lobes around landside access. Its airside face feeds Concourse T, a long
  transverse bar.
- **International Terminal outline:** a smaller, angular block at the east end, directly
  associated with the broad F concourse bar.
- **Relations:** T and A through F form a regimented rank of seven roughly parallel bars.
  A-E are detached from both processors at surface level; underground connections are not
  visible as black building footprints. The bars are similar in orientation but visibly
  unequal in length and end treatment. This is the clearest corpus example of terminal
  identity being defined by processors while most gates occupy separate satellites.

### KBOS — Boston Logan International

- **Chart count:** 4 passenger terminals: A, B, C, and E. **Category:** accreted unit
  terminal complex; every unit is a hybrid.
- **Terminal A outline:** a curved landside processor with a narrow neck to a rounded,
  crescent-like satellite. The satellite creates a dumbbell relationship rather than a
  simple pier.
- **Terminal B outline:** the broadest and most irregular unit, with a thick main mass,
  recessed courts, and projecting gate fingers on more than one face. It reads as two
  older halves consolidated into one uneven block.
- **Terminal C outline:** a curving processor with short piers and a narrow bridge toward
  the adjacent gate area; neither face is straight for long.
- **Terminal E outline:** a long crescent/boomerang international unit with a thicker
  middle and hooked ends.
- **Relations:** the four units wrap an incomplete airside arc around a central road and
  tower area. They share the same apron system but remain recognizable black silhouettes;
  cargo occupies a separate extension beyond E.

### KCLT — Charlotte Douglas International

- **Chart count:** 1 passenger terminal. **Category:** pier/comb hybrid.
- **Outline:** a thick central processor forms the spine. Multiple narrow concourses leave
  it at different angles and lengths, producing a ragged fan/comb rather than a regular
  E. Several roots have broad junction blocks and several tips widen or bend. The
  concourse family corresponds to A through E, including a newer splayed A-side branch.
- **Relations:** every passenger concourse is attached to the same processor, with apron
  alleys between fingers. The complex is strongly asymmetric: the longer, newer arms on
  one end oppose a denser set of older piers on the other. It is a useful correction to a
  generator that gives all piers one pitch and one orientation.

### KDCA — Ronald Reagan Washington National

- **Chart count:** 2 passenger terminals. **Category:** one small linear unit and one
  attached pier/comb unit.
- **Terminal 1 outline:** a compact, shallow linear/curvilinear block at the south end of
  the passenger apron. Its frontage follows the apron edge and has only modest projections.
- **Terminal 2 outline:** a long, narrow processor set parallel to the river/runway axis,
  with four short transverse gate fingers. The fingers are unequal, and the processor
  widens toward its landside end.
- **Relations:** Terminal 1 is detached from the south end of Terminal 2 by a small apron
  and circulation gap. Both sit on the same side of the runway system and read as a
  sequence, not a mirrored pair. Water and the constrained site explain the unusually
  linear arrangement.

### KDEN — Denver International

- **Chart count:** 1 passenger processor (Jeppesen Terminal). **Category:** processor plus
  parallel midfield satellites.
- **Processor outline:** a large rectangular/trapezoidal headhouse with stepped side
  additions and a comparatively flat airside face.
- **Concourse outlines:** A, B, and C are three detached, long, narrow bars with small
  central bulges and numerous tiny edge notches. Their lengths differ; B is the dominant
  middle bar, while the ends and extension phases are not identical.
- **Relations:** the processor and three concourses are parallel and widely spaced in one
  ordered stack. A is nearest the processor and has the visible bridge/tunnel relationship;
  B and C are fully detached in plan. Large dual-taxilane aprons occupy both faces of each
  bar. This is a satellite subtype, not four separate terminals.

### KDFW — Dallas Fort Worth International

- **Chart count:** 5 passenger terminals: A through E. **Category:** curvilinear unit
  terminals arranged on a spine.
- **Terminal A outline:** a deep C/horseshoe with a rounded airside rim, blunt open ends,
  and a landside court cut into the concave side.
- **Terminal B outline:** another C form, slightly tighter and more irregular, with end
  additions that disturb the nominal symmetry.
- **Terminal C outline:** a broad horseshoe with a large internal road void and a thicker
  central arc.
- **Terminal D outline:** the most block-like unit: a broad curved rectangle/crescent with
  substantial infill and less open concavity than A-C.
- **Terminal E outline:** a smaller horseshoe/crescent, visibly modified at its ends.
- **Relations:** the five units alternate along the central north-south roadway spine,
  with paired runway banks outside them. They repeat a planning concept but are not copies.
  The diagram shows why “semicircle” must include deep courts, end caps, bridges, and
  accreted width changes rather than a shallow arc band.

### KITH — Ithaca Tompkins International

- **Chart count:** 1 passenger terminal. **Category:** small linear terminal.
- **Outline:** a short rectangular processor on one edge of a compact apron, with several
  shallow gate-side projections that make the airside edge toothed. The landside edge is
  comparatively flat.
- **Relations:** the terminal is a single building with no detached concourse. Hangars,
  FBO, and fire-station buildings are separate and should not be folded into the terminal
  silhouette. This is a better small-airport target than a perfect unarticulated rectangle.

### KJFK — John F. Kennedy International

- **Chart count:** 5 passenger terminals visibly represented as the active numbered unit
  family: 1, 4, 5, 7, and 8. **Category:** heterogeneous unit terminals around a loop.
- **Terminal 1 outline:** a compact processor with several short radial gate arms, giving
  it a fan/star tendency rather than a bar.
- **Terminal 4 outline:** a large, elongated processor-and-concourse system with long arms
  and a bent/boomerang reading; its gate frontage reaches much farther than its neighbors.
- **Terminal 5 outline:** an irregular central mass with winged concourses, broad junction
  nodes, and a footprint that bends around the inner road system.
- **Terminal 7 outline:** a smaller, squarer unit with short opposed gate projections.
- **Terminal 8 outline:** a large U/comb hybrid with a broad processor and two dominant
  projecting concourse arms, plus shorter edge increments.
- **Relations:** the five units occupy different sides of a large rounded landside loop.
  Their orientations rotate around the center, so no common local “airside direction” can
  be applied to the entire complex. Large voids, roadway corridors, and former/under-
  construction areas separate the units.

### KLAS — Harry Reid International

- **Chart count:** 2 passenger processors, Terminals 1 and 3. **Category:** attached-pier
  plus satellite hybrid.
- **Terminal 1 outline:** an irregular, thick processor feeding the A, B, and C gate areas.
  Those areas form several short bars and branching fingers rather than one clean comb.
- **Terminal 3 outline:** a long, blocky linear processor with a comparatively straight
  face and the E-gate bar attached along its airside side.
- **Satellite outline:** D is a detached X/cruciform concourse with four rounded arms and

- **Relations:** Terminal 1 and Terminal 3 are separated along a common apron axis. D sits

### KLAX — Los Angeles International

- **Chart count:** 9 passenger processors—Terminals 1-8 and Tom Bradley International
  Terminal (TBIT)—plus the West Gates remote international concourse. **Category:**
  unit-terminal horseshoe plus remote linear satellite.
- **Terminal 1:** a compact wedge/hatchet with a tapered outer end. **Terminal 2:** a
  shallow stepped bar. **Terminal 3:** a longer, irregular bar with a widened gate end.
- **TBIT:** a broad central processor at the closed end of the U, with thick opposed
  concourse wings and a blockier outline than the numbered units.
- **Terminals 4-8:** five separate bars and L/T-like pieces down the opposite arm of the U;
  widths, lengths, and gate-side notches vary, with the outer units becoming narrower and
  more linear.
- **West Gates:** a long detached linear concourse west of TBIT, with repeated edge teeth
  and a narrow connection zone rather than a full landside processor.
- **Relations:** the nine processor units line the two arms and head of a U-shaped road
  system; their airside faces point outward. West Gates sits beyond the U and functions as
  a satellite. The overall silhouette is an accreted ring, not evenly pitched symbols.

### KMIA — Miami International

- **Chart count:** 3 named terminal sectors—North, Central, and South—within one largely
  continuous building system. **Category:** continuous pier/boomerang hybrid.
- **North Terminal outline:** dominated by the very long Concourse D bar, bent slightly
  and widened at several nodes; it forms one extended side of the complex.
- **Central Terminal outline:** a dense, irregular middle with the shorter E, F, and G
  projections. Infill at the roots makes the processor difficult to separate from the
  concourses.
- **South Terminal outline:** two strong H and J arms diverge from a thick joint, producing
  a V/fork at the opposite end from D.
- **Relations:** the three sectors form a single elongated arc around the landside area.
  This is a case where operational terminal count and number of disconnected black
  footprints do not agree; the correct generator target is one accreted mass with named
  sectors.

### KORD — Chicago O'Hare International

- **Chart count:** 4 passenger terminals: 1, 2, 3, and 5. **Category:** connected pier
  units plus a detached international linear unit.
- **Terminal 1 outline:** a broad processor feeding Concourse B and the detached parallel
  Concourse C; C is a long bar reached across the apron without a broad surface building.
- **Terminal 2 outline:** a compact processor with two principal E/F pier arms and uneven
  infill at their roots.
- **Terminal 3 outline:** the largest accreted comb, with G, H, K, and L fingers of
  different length, width, and angle; several junctions are nearly continuous black mass.
- **Terminal 5 outline:** a separate long curvilinear/linear international bar with a
  thick processor and gate frontage on a broad independent apron.
- **Relations:** Terminals 1-3 form a connected core beside the central runway bank.
  Terminal 5 is well removed across taxiway/runway space. The core is asymmetric and
  phased; it should not be reduced to three identical piers from one rectangle.

### KSEA — Seattle-Tacoma International

- **Relations:** the two satellites sit on opposite sides/ends of the central complex and

### KSFO — San Francisco International

### PANC — Ted Stevens Anchorage International

## Cross-corpus terminal rules supported by the images

1. **Count processors and describe concourses separately.** ATL, DEN, SEA, and KLAS have
   fewer terminals than prominent concourse buildings; MIA and Melbourne have named
   terminal sectors inside nearly continuous masses.
2. **Use hybrid categories freely.** At hub scale, a single pure category is the exception.
   “Pier,” “linear,” and “satellite” are usually the dominant component, not the whole
   description.
3. **A black component is not automatically a terminal.** Hangars, cargo terminals,
   maintenance halls, fire stations, and bridges can be larger or darker than a small
   passenger processor.
4. **Road geometry organizes unit terminals.** DFW, JFK, KLAX, and SFO cannot be generated
   convincingly by placing independent terminal glyphs at regular pitch; each unit rotates
   around or repeats along a landside spine.
5. **Apron negative space is part of the silhouette.** The gaps between piers and satellites
   contain taxilanes, service roads, holding areas, and pushback space. They are designed
   voids, not unused gray fill.
6. **Accretion breaks symmetry.** End additions, width changes, infilled pier roots, bent
   bars, and one-off pods occur at large and small airports. Exact repetition is mostly
   limited to a master-planned starting diagram and is later disturbed.
7. **Site constraints select the category.** Coastal/urban strips favor long linears
   (SBRJ, Gold Coast, Port Blair); midfield runway banks support parallel satellites (ATL,
   DEN); loop/spine road systems support unit terminals (DFW, JFK, KLAX, SFO).
